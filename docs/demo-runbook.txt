Internet Atlas: final demo

Pitch
Find a product opportunity in real customer requests, then find the people who asked for it.

Two-minute sequence
0:00 Open the atlas. Explain that this is attributed public evidence from HN, Reddit, GitHub, Software Recommendations and an opt-in X archive.
0:15 Ask: What business would you build from this data? Show measurable demand and a path to the first customers.
0:25 While Atlas works, explain the white rings and evidence trail. Semantic neighbors help navigation; only reviewed, distinct requester accounts contribute demand.
0:45 Show the product thesis and full highlighted cohort. Open one source to verify its actual request, date and author.
1:10 Choose Use this idea, then Find leads. Show that the second graph contains the actual requesters and separates recent from historical evidence.
1:30 Open one requester and draft a short message. Point out the concrete source detail, one question and editable draft. Nothing is sent.
1:55 Close: The output is a product hypothesis, the evidence behind it and a first interview list.

Expected judge questions
Who is it for? Technical founders seeking repeated product problems and people to interview.
What is different? A traceable path from original requests to a reviewed problem cohort, a product thesis and those same requesters as leads.
How is demand counted? Distinct public accounts making explicit requests within 365 days, deduplicated by account and original source. Votes and endorsements do not count. Cross-platform accounts may overlap.
What does AI do? It extracts literal request evidence, reviews same-problem membership, compares customer workflows, investigates alternatives and drafts concise outreach. Local embeddings handle retrieval.
What are the limits? Sampled coverage, inferred grouping, 614 candidates awaiting validation and no current cohort reaching 20 or 50. Requests do not establish purchases.
What about privacy? Public attribution and original dates are retained. Withdrawn sources are withheld, failures remain visible, contact details are not inferred and outreach stays in drafts.
What does it cost? No paid data API calls were used. Embeddings run locally. GPT 6 Luna uses the existing Codex subscription allowance.
